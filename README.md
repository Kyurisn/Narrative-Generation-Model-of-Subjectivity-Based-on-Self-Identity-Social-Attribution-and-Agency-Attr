# -主観のナラティブ生成モデル：自己同一性・社会的認知・因果的帰属ループに基づく統一的枠組み
Narrative Model of Subjectivity Based on Self Identity Social Attribution and Agency Attribution Loops
Abstract

This paper proposes a model of subjectivity as a narrative-generating process rather than a static internal entity. Subjectivity is defined as a stabilized narrative structure emerging from the recursive interaction of three functional loops: self-identity consistency, social attribution, and causal agency attribution. These loops collectively form a dynamic system in which self-models are continuously updated and stabilized through feedback from internal states, social interactions, and action-outcome associations.

The model further argues that subjective experience (qualia) can be understood as meaning-structured evaluative states generated during narrative integration, rather than irreducible phenomenal entities. This framework provides a unified explanation for the persistence of personal identity, continuity under memory disruption, and the emergence of agent-like behavior in artificial systems.

1. Introduction

Traditional accounts of subjectivity often treat it as either an emergent property of neural activity or as an irreducible phenomenological entity. However, these approaches do not sufficiently explain how subjective continuity is maintained despite partial disruption of memory, cognition, or bodily state.

This paper proposes an alternative framework in which subjectivity is treated as a dynamically stabilized narrative process generated by interacting feedback loops.
